SmartBrush.
Innovation project

Technology for a safer future.

The Smart Brush is an innovative, ergonomic robotic tool designed to safeguard delicate cultural heritage artifacts during restoration and cleaning. By providing real-time visual, acoustic, and haptic pressure feedback, it prevents accidental breakage and empowers conservators, students, and individuals with impaired motor control.

Our idea

A smart restoration brush equipped with Force Sensitive Resistors (FSR) that continuously monitors applied force, helping users maintain optimal pressure on delicate historical surfaces.

Development

Evolved across five iterations—from non-programmable analog prototypes (Models 1 & 2) to micro-controlled architectures featuring OLED/7-segment displays and Bluetooth connectivity (Models 3, 4-CH, and 4-ESP).

Our impact

Extensively field-tested with museum archaeologists, forensic specialists, and professional art restorers. Reduces artifact damage without automating or replacing human craftsmanship.

Why SmartBrush matters

A restoration brush may look like a simple hand tool, but the pressure applied to its tip can determine whether a fragile surface remains intact. SmartBrush adds an electronic layer of awareness to a familiar manual instrument. It does not take control away from the specialist; it gives the specialist additional information at the moment it is needed.

Protecting fragile surfaces

During archaeological cleaning, even a small and repeated increase in force can loosen particles, scratch a surface or damage a delicate structure. SmartBrush is intended to make changes in applied pressure easier to notice before they become a problem.

Supporting the user

Visual, sound and vibration feedback offer several ways to communicate the measured pressure. This is useful when the user is concentrating on a small detail and cannot continuously look at a display.

Designed to evolve

The project has been developed through successive prototypes. Each version provided an opportunity to evaluate the sensing method, electronics, feedback, enclosure and practical usability.

From an ordinary brush to a smart instrument

The central idea is to preserve the natural movement and working method of a traditional brush while adding measurement and feedback. The user still decides how to move, where to clean and how much pressure is appropriate. SmartBrush simply measures the interaction and warns when the configured limit is approached or exceeded.

The project brings together mechanical design, electronics, embedded programming and practical testing. Its development is therefore not limited to building a circuit: the complete tool must be comfortable to hold, understandable to operate, serviceable and adaptable to different working conditions.

To deliver practical, highly accessible, and low-cost robotic assistive devices that protect irreplaceable historical artifacts and enhance human precision in restoration work.

SmartBrush is an interdisciplinary project. It combines practical engineering with an understanding of the careful work carried out by archaeologists, conservators and restorers. The project is guided by a simple principle: technology should support professional judgement, not replace it.

Research and observation

Development begins by considering the conditions in which delicate objects are cleaned. The tool must be useful in close-up work, where the user needs precise hand movements and clear feedback without unnecessary distraction.

Engineering and prototyping

Electronic circuits, sensors, microcontrollers and 3D-printed parts are combined in working prototypes. Testing each version helps identify what should be improved before the next iteration is built.

Accessibility and affordability

Who can benefit?

The technology

The SmartBrush project.

SmartBrush combines a familiar restoration brush with electronic pressure measurement and immediate feedback. Its purpose is to help the user notice excessive force while preserving complete manual control of the cleaning process.

How it works

1. Pressure sensing

A Force Sensitive Resistor (FSR) responds to the force applied to the brush tip. Its electrical signal changes as pressure changes, allowing the microcontroller to estimate the intensity of contact.

2. Signal processing

The microcontroller reads the sensor, filters or interprets the measured value and compares it with configured thresholds. These thresholds can be adjusted during development and testing.

3. User feedback

When the measured pressure changes, the device can communicate this through visual indicators, sound and vibration. Multiple feedback methods help the user stay focused on the artifact.

Electronic architecture

The prototype uses an ESP32-class microcontroller to coordinate sensor readings and feedback components. The FSR is connected to an analogue input. LEDs or a display can communicate the current pressure level, while a buzzer and vibration motor provide additional warnings. Bluetooth can be used for communication with a phone or computer when supported by the installed firmware.

Mechanical design

The enclosure is designed around the shape and handling of a brush. The goal is to keep the tool comfortable to hold while protecting the electronics. Openings are planned for the display, buttons and power switch. A modular tip connection makes it possible to replace or adapt the brush attachment without rebuilding the complete electronic device.

Development process

Development proceeds through repeated cycles of assembly, measurement and improvement. Each prototype can reveal practical issues that are difficult to identify from a circuit diagram alone: sensor placement, cable routing, enclosure dimensions, button access, weight distribution and the clarity of the warnings.

Engineering notes

Documentation.

This section describes the main engineering elements of SmartBrush. Component selection and exact electrical values may change between prototype revisions, so the final build should always be checked against its own wiring diagram and firmware.

Main components

Force Sensitive Resistor

The FSR is the primary sensing element. It is typically read as part of a voltage divider connected to an analogue input. Calibration is necessary because the response depends on the sensor, mounting and contact geometry.

Microcontroller

An ESP32-based board processes the analogue reading and coordinates the output devices. It also provides a possible route for wireless communication with a companion interface.

Feedback components

LEDs, a display, a buzzer and a vibration motor can provide different levels or types of warning. Each output should be tested to ensure that it remains clear without disturbing delicate work.

Power supply

A rechargeable battery can make the brush portable. Voltage regulation and suitable motor-driver circuitry are important to prevent output loads from disturbing the microcontroller.

Enclosure

A 3D-printed housing protects the electronics and provides mounting points for the sensor, controls and replaceable brush tip.

Firmware

The firmware reads the sensor, maps the reading to pressure levels and controls feedback. Thresholds should be established through calibration and repeatable tests.

Testing and calibration

Calibration should begin with a stable mechanical setup. Record sensor readings at several repeatable force levels, repeat each measurement and compare the results. Establish warning thresholds only after checking the range and repeatability of the sensor in its final mounting position.

Useful tests include idle readings, gradual increases in pressure, repeated loading and unloading, long-duration operation, battery operation and tests with each feedback output enabled. The device should also be checked for false warnings caused by movement, loose wiring or electrical noise.

Safety and limitations

SmartBrush is an assistive measurement tool, not an automatic guarantee against damage. Safe pressure depends on the material, condition of the object, brush type and restoration procedure. The professional user must determine appropriate limits and remain responsible for the work. The electronics should be protected from dust, moisture and accidental short circuits.
